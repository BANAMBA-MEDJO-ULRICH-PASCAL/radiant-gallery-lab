## The problem

Certifications prove you studied. They don't prove you can spend someone else's
money well. I had the Google Ads, Analytics and Tag Manager certificates, and no
live account to point at.

So I built the proof myself: a real Search campaign, on a real website, with real
money. The constraint was the point — **just over $8 across five days**. A large
budget hides sloppy decisions behind volume. A micro-budget doesn't let you hide
anything.

## The approach

**Keywords chosen for intent, not volume.** I targeted terms where the searcher
is already looking to hire — `google ads management`, `google ads consultant`,
`optimize google ads` — rather than broad informational queries that would have
burned the budget on people who were only reading.

**Broad match, deliberately.** On $1.70 a day, exact match would have left the
ads barely surfacing at all. Broad match bought reach; the search terms report
was then monitored daily and negative keywords added to cut the irrelevant
queries it inevitably pulled in. That daily pruning is where the CTR gains came
from.

**Automation with the copy kept manual.** I used Google's AI Max to handle
delivery, but wrote all five sets of headlines and descriptions by hand — one
theme per buyer intent: management, setup, optimisation, conversion tracking and
remarketing. Machine learning decides *when* to show an ad far better than I can.
It should not be deciding what the business sounds like.

**Recommendations reviewed, not rubber-stamped.** Google's suggestions were
evaluated one at a time against the budget and the Maximise Clicks goal. Applying
them all would have been faster and worse.

## What the numbers say

A **12.11% CTR** on professional-services keywords is strong — those auctions are
competitive and expensive, and a $0.37 CPC on them reflects careful bid
management rather than luck. `optimize google ads` reached **33.33% CTR**, and
`google ads management` **15.69%**.

Equally useful is what *didn't* work. Five keywords — including `google ads
freelancer` and `google ads account audit` — recorded zero impressions. They
couldn't win an auction at this budget. That's not a failure to hide; it's the
clearest possible signal of where the first extra dollar should go.

**Traffic was ~75% mobile.** For any future client, that single fact reorders the
priorities: audit mobile landing-page performance on day one, and apply mobile
bid adjustments before touching anything else.

## Scope, and being straight about it

The objective here was **Maximise Clicks** — traffic and awareness for a brand-new
site with no organic presence. Conversion actions were deliberately not
configured, because on a five-day awareness campaign there would have been
nothing statistically meaningful to measure. The campaign is therefore proof of
traffic acquisition, not of cost-per-lead.

What GA4 did show is engagement past the landing page: of 11 users, the contact
page drew 3 views and the about page 1. People read on rather than bouncing —
a genuine signal of intent, but near-leads, not leads.

I also ran no A/B copy test. I had hypotheses about which value propositions
would land, and not nearly enough impressions to test them honestly.

The site the campaign pointed at was a WordPress build on Hostinger that I have
since retired, so there is no live link to show — only the account data above.

## What I'd do with a real budget

1. **Fund the starved keywords first** — five high-intent terms never entered the
   auction. That's the cheapest available win.
2. **A/B test the ad copy properly**, with enough impressions for the result to
   mean something.
3. **Add a remarketing layer.** The campaign sent visitors to the site with no
   way to re-engage them. That loop is currently open.
4. **Apply mobile bid adjustments and dayparting**, concentrating spend where the
   data already points.

Managing $10 carefully is a different skill from managing $10,000 carelessly —
and it's the one that transfers to a client's account.

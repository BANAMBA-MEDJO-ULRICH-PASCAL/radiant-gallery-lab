## The problem

Cameroonian music has an extraordinary history — Makossa, Bikutsi, Bend-Skin,
Assiko, Mangambeu, Ambasse Bey — and remarkably little of it is catalogued
anywhere structured. What exists is scattered across YouTube uploads, blog posts
and memory. There's no dataset you can ask questions of: which regions produced
which genres, how a style spread across decades, which languages artists actually
recorded in.

You can't analyse what nobody has collected. So Jackbeat is two things at once:
a collection instrument, and the analysis built on what it collects.

## The approach

**Model the domain honestly.** The hardest design decision was refusing generic
categories. Genre is not a free-text field — it's an explicit list of fourteen,
led by the styles that actually matter here rather than an international default.
Region covers all ten Cameroonian regions plus Diaspora. Language includes
Ewondo, Bassa, Duala, Fulfulde, Bamiléké, Pidgin English and Arabe Choa, because
a field offering only French and English would quietly erase most of the
catalogue.

**Crowdsource the input, but moderate it.** Anyone can submit an artist or a work
through a guided form. Nothing appears publicly on submission: every entry enters
as `en attente` and a moderator approves or rejects it. There's a reporting route
too, so errors in published entries can be flagged by readers.

Open contribution without moderation produces a fast-growing archive nobody can
cite. The review step is what makes the data worth analysing.

**Make the archive worth browsing.** Public pages for artists and works, search
with live suggestions, likes, comments, top charts, and YouTube links parsed from
any URL format so contributors don't have to care which one they paste.

**Then analyse it.** A dashboard built with pandas and Plotly: a regional map of
where artists come from, a chronology tracing output since independence,
aggregate statistics over genre and language, and an exportable PDF report —
because the people most likely to want this data are researchers and journalists,
who need something citable.

## Running it

Deployed on Render as infrastructure-as-code: a Flask service behind gunicorn,
a managed PostgreSQL database, and a health check. The admin password and session
secret are **generated by the platform** rather than committed — a small
discipline that matters, because a repository is forever and a leaked secret in
its history is very hard to take back.

## What I'd still like to do

The architecture is in place; what it needs now is **depth of catalogue**. A
music archive is only as valuable as the number of entries in it, and that comes
from sustained contribution and moderation rather than more code.

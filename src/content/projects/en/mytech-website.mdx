## The problem

A collection of small demos never answers the question a prospective client
actually has, which is not "can you style a button?" but **"can you build the
whole thing?"** A single page proves almost nothing. The difficulty in a real
website is not any individual screen — it is holding twenty decisions steady
across a dozen of them, so that page eleven still looks like it belongs beside
page one.

So I set myself the brief rather than waiting for it: a complete corporate site
for a fictional technology company, MyTech. Everything a company of that kind
actually needs, built to the point where only a server is missing.

## The approach

**Tokens before pages.** The first thing written was not a page, it was a
`:root` block: two brand colours, a six-step spacing scale, a seven-step type
scale, four shadows and three corner radii. Every rule after that consumes
those variables instead of literal values. It is a small discipline with a large
payoff — the palette and the rhythm of the whole site change from one block, and
nothing drifts, because no page has its own private idea of what "medium
spacing" means.

**Extract what repeats.** The header and footer live in `partials/header.html`
and `partials/footer.html` and are fetched at runtime into a placeholder
element. A navigation change is then one edit rather than twelve. The loader
also carries a hard-coded fallback header, so a failed fetch degrades to a
usable logo and link instead of a page with no way out.

**No libraries.** Every interaction is written by hand, which was the point of
the exercise: a sticky header that gains its shadow on scroll, anchor links that
scroll smoothly with an offset for that header, scroll-triggered reveals through
`IntersectionObserver`, statistics that count up when they come into view,
images deferred behind `data-src`, a scroll-to-top button, a toast notification
system, and a cookie-consent banner that stores the answer in `localStorage` so
it asks once and not again. The contact form validates its fields and the email
format client-side before it would ever reach a server.

**Accessibility where it costs nothing.** Labelled form controls, `aria-label`
on every icon-only control, alt text on all seventeen images, and a mobile menu
that closes on a link click, on an outside click, and restores body scroll when
it does.

## Where it stands

**The front end is complete; there is no backend.** That boundary is worth
stating plainly, because the site looks finished and is not:

- The login page accepts anything and forwards you to the dashboard. There is no
  authentication behind it.
- The dashboard is a real layout populated by **"Demo User"** and placeholder
  rows.
- The contact form validates properly, then simulates a send.
- MyTech is an invented company. The figures on the homepage — projects
  delivered, uptime, enterprise clients — are **placeholder copy for a fictional
  business**, not results of mine.

What that leaves is the part a backend cannot fake: structure, consistency and
interaction across twelve pages.

## What I took from it

Two things, one of them uncomfortable.

The useful one: **design tokens are what make a multi-page site cohere.** Once
spacing and type came from a scale rather than from judgement each time, pages
started matching each other without any effort to make them match.

The uncomfortable one: three of the twelve pages still carry their header inline
instead of using the partial, because I built those before extracting the
partial and never went back. It costs nothing today and it will cost something
the day the navigation changes. **Extract the repeating piece before you have
twelve copies of it, not after** — the refactor is cheap at page three and
tedious at page twelve.
